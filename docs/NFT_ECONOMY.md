# HAI NFT Economy

## Purpose

NFTs are an optional ownership, utility, access and collectible layer around Homeland Hunt. The core game should remain viable without requiring speculative asset appreciation.

## Asset Classes

### Functional Assets

- Shield
- Helmet
- Anchor
- Timepiece
- Maps / map fragments
- specialized clue or traversal tools

### Access Assets

- Legacy/member access
- special event eligibility
- early access
- premium viewing/access passes
- limited quest or NPC access

### Achievement / Collectible Assets

- leaderboard trophies
- artist collaboration badges
- Easter-egg discovery tokens
- seasonal commemoratives
- championship/retreat collectibles

## Primary Sales

HAI's stated strategy is to use a minting/ownership workflow such as Fogworks so HomelandAI can retain control over HAI-issued assets and capture first-sale economics rather than surrendering the asset relationship to an outside game publisher.

Primary revenue can come from:

- direct tool/map sales;
- bundles;
- limited event drops;
- artist collaborations;
- Legacy-related editions;
- earned assets that unlock optional upgrades.

## Secondary Sales & Royalties

Concept discussions used a **10% secondary royalty assumption** for scenario modeling. This is a planning assumption, not guaranteed enforceable revenue. Royalty enforcement varies by chain, marketplace, contract design and marketplace policy.

Conservative models discussed in development assumed modest resale velocity and low blended resale prices. Those numbers should be rebuilt from real cohort data after beta rather than used as financial promises.

## Holder Economics

Possible holder value can include:

- use in gameplay;
- resale where supported;
- limited rental/licensing mechanics if technically and legally appropriate;
- access benefits;
- status/collection value.

Do not market holder income as guaranteed, passive income, yield or investment return. Any revenue-sharing or staking structure requires separate legal analysis.

## Design Principle: Utility Before Speculation

A Shield should be valuable because its gameplay or access function is desirable. The same principle applies to Helmets, Anchors, Timepieces and maps. Scarcity should amplify utility—not replace it.

## Anti-Pay-to-Win Controls

- cap tool uses per event;
- provide earnable alternatives;
- separate convenience from decisive competitive advantage;
- disclose effects before purchase;
- balance high-value cash events especially carefully;
- log every tool activation for auditability.

## Long-Term Opportunity

The strongest evolution is to make premium tools persistent AI companions. A Timepiece can become a Strategist; Helmet an Analyst; Shield a Guardian; Anchor a Pathfinder. Ownership then represents an evolving digital teammate rather than a static collectible.
